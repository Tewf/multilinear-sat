# The algorithm as measured

The surviving arm of [../benchmark/arms/](../benchmark/arms/README.md), written as pseudocode
with, after each choice, the number that made it (from [front.md](../benchmark/arms/front.md)
and [rejected.md](../benchmark/arms/rejected.md), 2026-08-29 and the n = 5000 and
cross-entropy stages of 2026-09-01: expected time to a solution in ms,
the batch on one RTX 4060 Laptop GPU against probSAT on one core, uf250-1065 unless a family is
named). The design as proposed is [anytime-las-vegas.md](anytime-las-vegas.md) and
[sampling-gradient-loop.md](sampling-gradient-loop.md); the method as built is
[README.md](README.md). This file is regenerated by hand whenever the front changes.

## The front

Seven arms no other dominates: the plain uniform walk (**base**), the same walk from a 10- or
30-step ascent (**ascent_10**, **ascent_30**), the same walk at 16384 slots
(**batch_16384**), the same walk with 100n flips per unit at n = 1000 (**polish_100n**), and
the two crosses, which add nothing to their single factors: the 10-step ascent with the 100n
walk (262 ms against 256 at n = 1000, and 24.2 s against 22.9 at n = 5000, both inside the
noise; at n = 5000 the ascent turned one instance's zero positive, s3 at p = 2 to 3e-4, and
the family cell still does not distinguish) and the 16384 batch with the 20n polish
on uf250 (0.120 against 0.117, a tie on time, p 0.38 against 0.23).
Nothing beats the plain uniform walk beyond noise on any family it was measured on at the
same walk length; the batch size wins on uf250 alone and loses at n = 1000; the short ascent
is not distinguishable from a uniform start on expected time anywhere and better on p on one
family; and the walk's length is the one lever that moves the order of magnitude, 35x at
n = 1000. So the algorithm is the plain uniform walk with restarts and a posterior, whose
unit grows with n, with the batch size and the short ascent as the two knobs the front keeps.

    solve(F with n variables, m rows, time limit T):
        B <- 16384 while B (n + 9 m) bytes fit the card's cache, else 4096
              uf250: 0.117 ms per solution at 16384 against 0.224 at 4096 (1.9x), and 0.120

              4.20: not distinguished

              satisfied slot-runs, 22.9 s expected) and still loses 11x to probSAT's 2.06 s

              against no restart (twelve units in one run): 0.224 against 0.389

## The posterior's line

The posterior is a clock, not a test: it depends on the failure count alone once the prior is
fixed, reads the same on satisfiable and unsatisfiable instances at every run, and crosses
0.99 at the 42nd failed run on every uuf250 instance, 8 s at full speed against kissat's 2.5 s
refutation ([../benchmark/findings-walk/posterior.md](../benchmark/findings-walk/posterior.md)).
It stays in the algorithm because the alternative is nothing: the solver never claims UNSAT,
and the number is what a caller gets instead of a verdict.

## What stays optional, and why

- **The rigorous fraction** (half the batch walking Schöning's rule from uniform starts for 3n
  flips): 0.396 against 0.224 on uf250, the certificate costs 1.77x, and its bound
  (3/4)^n / (3(3n + 1)) cannot move at n = 250. Off by default; it is the honest half for a
  caller who wants a one-sided error bound and a small n.
- **The ascent seed** (10 steps): on the front, not distinguishable from a uniform start on
  expected time, better on p at n = 1000, 4.26 only. Off by default until a family shows it
  beyond the noise; the 50- and 200-step seeds are rejected (0.256 and 0.429 on uf250).
- **Parity mode**: a parity is one row of the same formula, its flip a toggle. Measured on
  MM-Challenge-1 ([../benchmark/findings-walk/parities.md](../benchmark/findings-walk/parities.md)):
  none of ten solved where xnfsat solves three, so it is a capability, not a claim. The xnf
  rule (xnfSAT's weighted break, 2026-09-01) halves the violated floor on every instance and
  still solves none at this budget shape; the residue tracks chain length, not the score.
- **The tilted seed**: rejected on the sampling-walk records, 94x a uniform start's expected
  time on the runs it completed.
- **The cross-entropy loop** (the tilted loop with SKC rungs as elite generator,
  `--tilted-skc-rungs`, priced 2026-09-01 at n = 1000, 4.20): the same seven satisfied
  slot-runs as the base at 9.1x its cost per restart, 82.2 s expected against 9.0, dominated
  by every arm on the family. The Python walk-mode loop's 17x loss survives the batched
  kernel as 9.1x, which closes the hand-off's second test in the negative.

## Against probSAT on one core

uf50 0.006 against 10.4 ms; uf100 0.029 against 11.7; uf250 0.117 (16384 slots) against 24.2;
n = 1000 at 4.20: 9.0 s against 1.85 s at 10n flips per unit (the walk loses: seven satisfied
slot-runs in 229 376, the cutoff hundreds of times shorter than the 10 600n flips probSAT
spends per solution) and 0.256 s at 100n (the walk wins 7x, at 16 M flips per solution
against probSAT's 10.6 M: the batch's throughput, not a better walk);
n = 1000 at 4.26: 35 to 39 ms against a 2.9 s mean and 76 ms median on the three decided
instances; n = 5000 at 4.20: 22.9 s at the 100n unit (the 10n unit found nothing in 200n
flips per slot) against 2.06 s, an 11x loss that the walk's length narrows but does not
close. The batch wins
where a restart of 10n flips has a per-restart success above about 1e-4 and loses where the
walk needs thousands of n flips uninterrupted.

## What remains

The long walk at n = 5000 ran on 2026-09-01 (with and without the ascent; the cells are
above), which closes the hand-off's first test: the walk's length turns the family's zero
into p = 8.1e-4 and the batch still loses 11x on expected time, so the loss boundary of the
previous section stands with its number. What is still open: the unit that pays is measured
at three points (10n to n = 250, 100n at n = 1000 and n = 5000), a schedule, not a law, and
nothing between 10n and 100n was priced; n = 5000 at 4.26 has no decided instance (probSAT
capped at 120 s on all five, CaDiCaL without a verdict), so no arm can be priced there until
one is decided; and the parity route's xnf rule
([../benchmark/findings-walk/parities.md](../benchmark/findings-walk/parities.md)) is priced
outside the arms protocol, on MM-Challenge-1 directly.
